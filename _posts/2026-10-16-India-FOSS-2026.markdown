# My First INDIA FOSS 2026  


## Introduction

[The FOSS United Foundation](https://fossunited.org/), founded in 2020, is a non-profit organization dedicated to fostering Free and Open Source Software (FOSS) projects and communities in India.

This was my first time attending a conference like IndiaFOSS, and I had no idea what to expect. However, I was extremely excited about the event. Who is behind FOSS, and who are the people making it happen? Also, why is FOSS important for students?

## Key Themes and Sessions

The conference featured Devrooms,Workshops,Booths & Community Partners,Communi-Con.  

### [**Devrooms:**](https://fossunited.org/indiafoss/2026/devrooms)

These were selected from an enthusiastic community response to the devroom idea. Devrooms are curated by various communities, represented by the managers of the individual devrooms. Each devroom has a separate CFP that defines the type and kind of proposals the devroom is looking for. 

I attented ***OPEN HARDWARE*** Devroom **Build your own open source keyboard with ZMK/QMK** by [Advait Dhamorikar](https://www.linkedin.com/in/advait-dhamorikar/?isSelfProfile=false)

### Key Takeways:
- Understand how modern keyboards work, from matrix scanning to USB/Bluetooth HID.
- Learn the workflow for designing a custom keyboard, from schematic and PCB design to firmware development.
- Understand how layers, combos, macros, and custom behaviors can improve your workflow.

## Three sessions stood out to me as particularly impactful:

### 1. From Institution to Community  by  [Mohit P. Tahiliani](https://www.linkedin.com/in/mohittahiliani/?isSelfProfile=false)
This talk will share the journey of building COSH as one of India's first dedicated academic centers focused on open-source software and hardware, and how it evolved from an institutional initiative into a thriving community of contributors, maintainers, researchers, and industry collaborators.

* He explained how to build a structured pathway for students to move from first-time contributors to open-source maintainers within an academic institution?
* Approaches for building a self-sustaining ecosystem where students evolve into mentors, maintainers, and community leaders over time.

Things I was searching for a long time. How to connect our learing to workforce. COSH is doing that kind of stuff.

### 2. [Textbooks that run ](https://kcsrk.info/ocaml/teaching/nptel/llm/2026/09/26/textbooks-that-run/)interactive CS education in the browser by [KC Sivaramakrishnan](https://kcsrk.info/)

One of the biggest obstacles a beginner faces with a programming language, especially one outside the mainstream, is the install: toolchains, package managers, editor setup, and version skew that derail a learner before they write a line of code.This talk is about a course book that removes that barrier

The book reads like a notebook, and a single markdown source produces all three views: the page, a reveal.js slide deck, and the runnable cells, so the slides cannot drift from the prose. Quizzes are built into the same format: multiple-choice with explanations, and code-completion challenges whose hidden tests run in the browser.

He also explained about how use of agent helping to Building this

### 3. 15 Years, One Server  by [lakshmipathi ganapathi](https://github.com/Lakshmipathi)
This talk provides insights about running large educational platform with minimal resources and  how it survived for 15 years on a single Linux server with 8GB ram and helped more than 500,000 users to learn and practice Linux. 

He share lessons from their infra failures, maintenance and challenges of keeping it alive and free for 15 years.
